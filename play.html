Mirror Magic

Symmetry10 min

"Symmetry is everywhere: spider webs, leaves, even your face! Let's find the line where the two halves match."

Video coming soon Spider shows the fold-and-paint trick · about 60 sec

The big idea

  1. Fold a picture in half. If both sides match, it has symmetry.Butterflies have it. So does your face!
  2. The fold line is the mirror.One side is the flip of the other.
  3. Count squares from the line.2 steps to the left? Its twin is 2 steps to the right. Same row, same color.
Make it with art

Fold a paper in half and open it. Paint blobs on one side only. Fold it shut, press, and open it. A butterfly!

For grown-ups: lines of symmetry (Common Core 4.G.A.3), with an early start for grades 1–3.

Game: Finish the picture

Pick a level

The left half is done. Paint the right half so it mirrors the left. Pick a color, then tap squares.

Stop 2 · Hosted by the Environmental Engineer

Honeycomb Hunt

Shapes & anglesEnvironmental engineering15 min

"Bees are nature's builders! Their hexagons fit together with no gaps. Which other shapes can do that?"

Video coming soon Bee traces a honeycomb · about 60 sec

The big idea

  1. Some shapes fit together with no gaps.Floor tiles do it. So does a honeycomb.
  2. Where the corners meet, they go all the way around.All the way around is 360 degrees. Degrees are how we measure a turn.
  3. Bees use hexagons because they fit together and use less wax.Less wax to build means more honey to eat!
Make it with art

Cut a hexagon out of a cereal box. Trace it, slide it over so one edge touches, and trace again. Keep going until you have a honeycomb, then color it in.

For grown-ups: angles as parts of a 360° turn (Common Core 4.MD.C.5), adding angles (4.MD.C.7), and comparing design solutions (NGSS 3-5-ETS1-2).

Game: Which shapes fit together?

Pick a shape. Add copies around the dot and watch the corners add up to 360°, which is all the way around. Then decide: do they fit with no gaps?

Stop 3 · Hosted by the Architect

The Trinket Box

3D shapesArchitecture15 min

"Every box starts out flat. Let's fold some up and see which ones work!"

Video coming soon Beaver folds a cereal box into a trinket box · about 60 sec

The big idea

  1. A box is made of 6 flat squares.Look at any box. Count the sides.
  2. Lay the squares out flat, then fold them up.Do they close into a box?
  3. Some flat shapes work. Some leave a hole.Try folding and see!
Make it with art

Draw a cross of six squares on a flattened cereal box. Cut it out, fold on the lines, and tape the edges. Decorate it before you fold. Now you have a trinket box!

For grown-ups: nets of three-dimensional shapes (Common Core 6.G.A.4, started early) and spatial reasoning.

Game: Fold or flop?

Look at the flat shape. Guess if it folds into a closed box, then watch it fold up for real.

Shape 1 of 8. Will it fold into a box?

Robot Painter

CodingStep by step15 min

"A program is just a list of steps. My robot does exactly what I say, so I have to say it exactly right."

Video coming soon Python programs a friend to draw a house · about 60 sec

The big idea

  1. A program is a list of steps.The robot does exactly what the list says.
  2. Little steps add up.Move, paint, move, paint.
  3. Did it go wrong? That's a bug.Fix one step and run it again.
Make it with art

Write 8 steps for drawing a house. Give them to a friend who has to follow them exactly. Did the house come out right? Where was the bug?

For grown-ups: sequences and debugging (CSTA K-12 standards 1A-AP-10 and 1A-AP-14).

Stop 5 · Hosted by the Physicist

Ramp Racer

EnergyFriction15 min

"Why does a ball roll farther off a tall ramp? And why does carpet stop it so fast? Let's test it and find out!"

Video coming soon Dolphin races marbles down a cardboard ramp · about 60 sec

The big idea

  1. A tall ramp gives the ball lots of go.A short ramp gives it just a little.
  2. The floor slows the ball down.Bumpy carpet slows it a lot. Slippery ice barely slows it.
  3. Change one thing at a time.Then you know what made the difference.
Make it with art

Make a ramp from a cereal box and some books. Roll a marble onto paper, then a towel, then the floor. Put a sticker where it stops each time.

For grown-ups: energy and speed (NGSS 4-PS3-1), forces and motion (3-PS2-1), and fair tests.

Game: Land in the zone

Each block of ramp height rolls the ball a few blocks along the floor: 3 on ice, 2 on wood, 1 on carpet. Pick a height and a floor so the ball stops in the green zone.

Stop 6 · Hosted by the Aerospace Engineer

Paper Plane Lab

FlightTesting20 min

"I toss everything to see if it flies. Then I ask why. Today we're tossing paper planes!"

Video coming soon Falcon tests three planes down a hallway · about 60 sec

The big idea

  1. Wings push air down, and the air pushes the wing up.That push is called lift.
  2. Point the nose up a little for more lift.Point it way up and the plane stalls and drops.
  3. A paper clip on the nose keeps it steady.Too many clips and it dives.
Make it with art

Fold a paper plane and decorate the wings. Throw it 5 times with no clip and 5 times with a clip on the nose. Which goes farther?

For grown-ups: forces on objects (NGSS 3-PS2-1) and testing designs (3-5-ETS1-3).

Game: Fly through the hoop

Set up the plane so it flies through the hoop. Each choice tells you what it does. Your last flight stays on screen as a dotted line so you can see what to change.

Stop 7 · Hosted by the Mechanical Engineer

Gear Garage

MachinesRatios15 min

"Gears are my favorite. Turn one and the whole machine wakes up. Let's build a gear train!"

Video coming soon Octopus builds cardboard gears · about 60 sec

The big idea

  1. Gears are wheels with teeth.Turn one and it turns the next.
  2. Gears that touch spin opposite ways.
  3. Small gear turning a big gear: slower.Big gear turning a small gear: faster. Count the teeth!
Make it with art

Trace a bottle cap, a mug, and a bowl on cardboard. Cut teeth around the edges. Pin them to a box so the teeth touch. Turn one and watch the others go!

For grown-ups: simple machines, ratios as a first look (Common Core 6.RP.A.1), and engineering design (NGSS 3-5-ETS1-1).

Game: Spin the fan

The handle turns the first gear. The fan is on the last gear. Pick gears so the fan does what the challenge says.
